Special Inspector General Requirements (GR)

High-level educational outline only. Study the official Special Inspection Manual / IBC Chapter 17 and current GR content outline.

Purpose & significance

The special inspector is an extra set of qualified eyes for critical structural elements. The building official relies on the special inspection program to verify that the work matches the approved construction documents.

Key roles

  • Special Inspector — observes, documents, reports non-conformance
  • Registered Design Professional — prepares the statement of special inspections
  • Building Official — approves the special inspector and the overall program
  • Contractor — performs the work and is responsible for quality

Continuous vs Periodic

Know which items require the inspector to be present the entire time the work is being performed (continuous) versus those that can be checked at intervals (periodic). Most reinforced concrete placement and reinforcement installation fall under continuous inspection.

Reporting

  • Daily reports of what was inspected
  • Immediate notification of non-conformance
  • Final report when the work is complete

Common exam traps: Confusing the special inspector’s duties with the contractor’s quality control, or thinking the special inspector “approves” the work instead of verifying and reporting.

Concrete Quality, Mix Verification & Testing

Builds directly on your ACI Field Grade I knowledge. The special inspector observes and verifies that the required testing is performed correctly.

Key inspection points

  • Verify the delivered mix matches the approved mix design (batch tickets)
  • Confirm time limits, water added, and consistency
  • Observe sampling (C172), temperature (C1064), slump (C143), air content, and cylinder fabrication (C31)
  • Ensure specimens are properly protected for initial curing

You already know the detailed field procedures from CP-1. On the ICC exam the emphasis shifts to verification that the testing program is being followed and recognizing when results or procedures are non-conforming.

Reinforcement

High-level educational outline. Study ACI 318 Chapter 26 (or current construction requirements chapter), CRSI Field Inspection of Reinforcing Bars, and the approved plans.

What the inspector verifies

  • Bar size, grade, and type match the plans
  • Bars are free of excessive rust, oil, dirt, or damage
  • Placement (location, spacing, clear distances)
  • Concrete cover (critical for durability and fire resistance)
  • Lap splices, mechanical splices, or welded splices are correct length/type
  • Supports, chairs, and bolsters maintain position during placement
  • Hooks, bends, and development lengths are as detailed

Common pitfalls: Measuring cover after the pour, accepting bars with heavy flaky rust, missing that a lap is in the wrong location or of insufficient length, or failing to check that the bars will not be displaced when concrete is placed and vibrated.

Formwork, Embeds, Joints & Curing

Formwork & embeds

  • Formwork must be constructed so that the finished concrete matches the required dimensions, alignment, and surface finish
  • Verify embeds, anchor bolts, sleeves, and other items are in the correct location and elevation before concrete is placed
  • Form removal timing affects strength and surface quality

Joints

Construction joints, isolation joints, and contraction joints must be located and prepared as shown on the plans or as approved.

Curing

  • Hot weather and cold weather concreting have specific temperature and protection requirements
  • Curing method (water, membrane, etc.) must be appropriate and maintained for the required duration
  • Early-age temperature and moisture control have a major effect on long-term strength and durability

Codes Module Focus (47C)

The Codes exam tests your ability to apply IBC Chapter 17 special inspection requirements and the referenced technical standards to reinforced concrete construction.

Core themes that appear repeatedly

  • Statement of special inspections — what must be inspected and at what frequency
  • Responsibilities of the special inspector vs other parties
  • When continuous inspection is required
  • Verification of materials (concrete, reinforcement, anchors)
  • Documentation and non-conformance reporting
  • Relationship between the IBC and ACI 318 / ACI 301

Study the current content outline published by ICC and work through the Concrete Manual and ACI 318 construction requirements systematically.

Plans Reading Module (47P)

Open-book plan-reading exam. Success comes from systematic verification, not memorization.

Typical verification sequence

  1. Review the structural notes and general notes first
  2. Confirm the statement of special inspections requirements
  3. Check formwork dimensions, elevations, and embeds against the drawings
  4. Verify reinforcement: size, spacing, cover, splices, hooks, and support
  5. Confirm that the concrete mix and testing frequency match the project requirements
  6. Document any discrepancy before concrete is placed

Common plan-reading traps: Missing a note that changes cover or splice length in a specific location, overlooking that a bar is called out as epoxy-coated, or failing to notice that the section cut does not match the plan view.
