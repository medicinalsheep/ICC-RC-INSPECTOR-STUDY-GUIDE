The natural next step after ACI Concrete Field Testing Technician—Grade I.

  2. ICC Special Inspector General Requirements (GR)

Special Inspector General Requirements (GR)

High-level educational outline only. Study the official Special Inspection Manual / IBC Chapter 17 and current GR content outline. Verify details in current official references.

Purpose & significance

The special inspector is an extra set of qualified eyes for critical structural elements. The building official relies on the special inspection program to verify that the work matches the approved construction documents.

Key roles

  • Special Inspector — observes, documents, reports non-conformance
  • Registered Design Professional — prepares the statement of special inspections
  • Building Official — approves the special inspector and the overall program
  • Contractor — performs the work and is responsible for quality

Continuous vs Periodic

Know which items require the inspector to be present the entire time the work is being performed (continuous) versus those that can be checked at intervals (periodic). Most reinforced concrete placement and reinforcement installation fall under continuous inspection. Confirm the statement of special inspections and IBC Ch. 17 tables for the project — do not guess.

Reporting

  • Daily reports of what was inspected
  • Immediate notification of non-conformance
  • Final report when the work is complete

Common exam traps: Confusing the special inspector’s duties with the contractor’s quality control, or thinking the special inspector “approves” the work instead of verifying and reporting.

Concrete Quality, Mix Verification & Testing

Builds directly on your ACI Field Grade I knowledge. On the ICC pathway the emphasis shifts from performing every field test yourself to verifying that the testing program is followed, that the delivered concrete matches the approved design, and that non-conformances are caught before they become permanent. Soft themes below — verify procedures and acceptance criteria in current official references / ACI standards / project specs.

JTA-style angles (what “good” looks like)

  • Can you explain why a batch ticket must match the approved mix before any concrete enters the forms?
  • Can you describe the sampling location and frequency expectations for the pour you are watching?
  • Do you know what to do if water is added at the site, or if discharge time looks excessive?
  • Can you spot an invalid cylinder set (wrong mold, bad rod/vibration, poor initial cure protection)?

Inspection sequence — before the first truck

  1. Confirm approved mix design(s) and any placement notes (slump/air windows, admixtures, fiber, lightweight, etc.).
  2. Confirm the statement of special inspections / testing frequency for this pour.
  3. Confirm who is performing field tests (qualified technician) and where specimens will be stored for initial curing.
  4. Walk the placement path: access, pump line cleanliness, cold joints from prior placements, weather protection plan.

During delivery & placement

  • Batch tickets: Mix ID / design designation, time batched, volume, water (batch + any additions), admixtures. Flag mismatches immediately.
  • Time & consistency: Watch elapsed time from batching to placement and any site-added water. Project specs and ASTM practices set limits — verify in current references; do not invent a number from memory on exam day.
  • Observe testing: Sampling (C172-family), temperature (C1064), slump (C143), air content method appropriate to the mix, cylinder/beam fabrication (C31). You are verifying the program — know when results or methods look non-conforming.
  • Placement & consolidation: Layer thickness, vibration technique, avoiding segregation and cold joints, protecting reinforcement position.

After placement (same day / early age)

  • Confirm initial curing of specimens: moisture, temperature band, protected location — bad early cure can invalidate strength evaluation.
  • Confirm curing of the structure starts as required (see Formwork/Curing module).
  • Document truck counts, test results, weather, start/stop times, and any deviations.

Common non-conformances

  • Wrong mix or wrong plant ticket for the element being placed
  • Excessive site water without documentation / approval path
  • Tests skipped, sampled from the wrong place, or performed with contaminated equipment
  • Cylinders left in sun/wind or without moisture — initial cure failure
  • Placement continuing after a failed acceptance check without design professional direction
  • Honeycomb / voids from inadequate consolidation or blocked forms

Edge cases & judgment

  • Multiple mixes on one pour day — ticket discipline becomes critical
  • Hot or cold weather: temperature of concrete and ambient conditions interact with set time and finishing
  • Pumped concrete: line priming slurry must not contaminate structural concrete
  • Small “makeup” loads late in the pour — still need identification and testing rules applied
Documentation instinct: If you cannot prove what mix went where, what tests were run, and what you observed, the inspection record is incomplete. Write it while it is fresh — not “tomorrow.”

Verify acceptance windows, sampling rates, and temperature limits in current official references / ACI 318 / project specs / ASTM standards — this file intentionally avoids hard copyrighted tables.

Reinforcement

High-level educational outline. Study ACI 318 construction requirements (current edition), CRSI Field Inspection of Reinforcing Bars, and the approved plans. Soft themes — verify cover, spacing, and splice rules in current official references / ACI 318 / IBC as applicable.

JTA-style angles

  • Can you walk a mat and systematically confirm size → grade/coating → spacing → cover → splices → supports?
  • Do you know what must be correct before concrete arrives versus what you can only document as a deficiency after?
  • Can you explain why cover is a durability and fire-resistance issue, not just a “code number”?

What the inspector verifies

  • Bar size, grade, and type (including coatings / stainless / headed bars) match the plans
  • Bars free of excessive flaky rust, mill scale that interferes with bond, oil, mud, or damage
  • Placement: location, spacing, layering, clear distances between bars and bundles
  • Concrete cover to form faces, earth, and exposed surfaces as detailed
  • Lap splices, mechanical splices, or welded splices — type, location, length/qualification
  • Supports, chairs, bolsters, and ties that will hold position during placement and vibration
  • Hooks, bends, development, and anchorage as detailed (including seismic hooks where required)
  • Dowels and future-pour bars: embedment, protection, and alignment

Inspection sequence — before pour

  1. Review structural notes, schedules, and typical details (cover, splice preferences, coating notes).
  2. Confirm mill certificates / identification when required by the project documents.
  3. Check formwork geometry first — wrong forms make “correct” bar placement impossible.
  4. Verify primary bars (beams, columns, walls, slabs) against plans; then secondary / distribution steel.
  5. Measure cover and clear spacing at critical locations (corners, congested joints, below grade faces).
  6. Confirm splices are not clustered in high-stress regions contrary to the details.
  7. Confirm embeds and openings will not force bars out of position when concrete is placed.
  8. Document and resolve deficiencies before concrete is ordered / discharged.

After / during placement (still your problem)

  • Watch for displacement when workers walk mats or when the pump hose / chute hits the cage.
  • Vibration should consolidate concrete without permanently shifting bars or collapsing chairs.
  • If bars are pushed into inadequate cover, stop and correct — do not “cover it up” with more concrete.

Common non-conformances

  • Wrong bar size or grade substituted “because it was on the truck”
  • Insufficient cover at earth faces, formed faces, or fire-rated assemblies
  • Lap length short, or lap in the wrong zone (e.g., midspan vs support as detailed)
  • Missing ties / stirrups / seismic hooks
  • Bars floating or sagging — inadequate support spacing or wrong chair height
  • Heavy flaky rust or oil-coated bars accepted without cleanup
  • Bundled bars with inadequate clear spacing for consolidation

Edge cases

  • Congested beam-column joints: clear spacing and consolidation path matter as much as bar count
  • Epoxy-coated bars: coating damage, patching, and compatible ties/chairs
  • Mechanical couplers: installation torque / inspection marks per manufacturer and project specs
  • Post-pour discovery of low cover — usually a deficiency report, not a field “fix”
Documentation instinct: Record locations checked (grid lines / elevations), measured cover samples, splice types observed, and unresolved items. Photos help when allowed by project protocol.

Verify cover tables, splice multipliers, and development rules in current official references / ACI 318 / CRSI — do not memorize a single number from an unofficial guide.

Formwork, Embeds, Joints & Curing

High-level educational outline. Formwork must produce the geometry the engineer designed; embeds must be right before the pour; curing protects the investment. Verify timing, tolerances, and temperature practices in current official references / ACI guides / project specs.

JTA-style angles

  • Can you separate “formwork safety/engineering” (often the contractor’s PE) from “geometry and cleanliness the SI verifies against plans”?
  • Do you have a pre-pour embed checklist mindset (anchors, sleeves, welds, elevation, projection)?
  • Can you describe what changes in hot vs cold weather for protection and curing?

Formwork — what to check before pour

  • Dimensions, alignment, elevations, and camber as shown on the drawings
  • Form tightness and cleanliness — debris, ice, standing water, and bond-breakers where specified
  • Form release agents compatible with the finish and not contaminating reinforcement
  • Openings, blockouts, and construction joint locations match the plans or approved changes
  • Adequate bracing/ties so forms will not bulge under fluid concrete pressure (observe for movement during placement)

Embeds & miscellaneous steel

  • Anchor bolts, plates, sleeves, conduits, and weldments: location, elevation, projection, and orientation
  • Templates and jigs that hold embeds during vibration
  • Coordination conflicts: rebar vs embed — resolve before concrete, not after
  • After pour, verify projections and clean threads only if still accessible; misplaced embeds usually need NCR + design input

Joints

Construction joints, isolation joints, and contraction joints must be located and prepared as shown on the plans or as approved. Preparation often includes cleaning laitance, roughening as specified, and continuing reinforcement through the joint when detailed. Waterstops (if any) need continuity and correct position before concrete covers them.

Curing & weather protection

  • Why cure: Moisture and temperature control drive early hydration, strength gain, reduced plastic shrinkage, and durability.
  • Methods: Continuous water, wet covers, membrane-forming compounds, curing papers/blankets — method must suit the surface and exposure.
  • Hot weather themes: Rapid set, plastic shrinkage, high concrete temperature — cooling, wind breaks, timely finish/cure. Verify limits in current refs.
  • Cold weather themes: Protection from freezing, adequate strength before exposing to cold, heated enclosures or insulated blankets as specified. Verify limits in current refs.
  • Duration: Follow project specs / referenced standards — “overnight and strip” is not a curing plan.

Form removal & reshores (high-level)

  • Removal timing affects finish and structural safety; follow plans/specs and strength criteria when specified.
  • Reshores may be required to support construction loads on multi-story work — this is a structural sequencing issue; flag deviations.

Common non-conformances

  • Forms out of tolerance (bulging walls, low slab thickness, wrong elevation)
  • Dirt, sawdust, or ice left in forms
  • Embeds shifted during placement; anchor patterns wrong for base plates
  • Construction joint in the wrong place or poorly prepared
  • Curing compound applied too late, too thin, or on a surface that needed wet curing
  • Early form strip exposing green concrete to freezing or drying wind

Edge cases

  • Architectural concrete: tighter finish and repair criteria — document defects early
  • Mass concrete: temperature control plans may apply — SI verifies monitoring is occurring as specified
  • Night pours / windy decks: evaporation control becomes critical even if ambient air is mild
Before vs after pour: Geometry, embeds, joint prep, and release conditions are cheap to fix before concrete. Afterward they become NCRs, repairs, or redesign. Prioritize the irreversible checks.

Verify tolerances, curing durations, and hot/cold weather temperature bands in current official references / ACI 305R / 306R / 308 / 347 / project specs.

Codes Module Focus (47C)

The Codes exam tests your ability to apply IBC Chapter 17 special inspection requirements and the referenced technical standards to reinforced concrete construction.

Core themes that appear repeatedly

  • Statement of special inspections — what must be inspected and at what frequency
  • Responsibilities of the special inspector vs other parties
  • When continuous inspection is required
  • Verification of materials (concrete, reinforcement, anchors)
  • Documentation and non-conformance reporting
  • Relationship between the IBC and ACI 318 / ACI 301

Study the current content outline published by ICC and work through the Concrete Manual and ACI 318 construction requirements systematically.

Plans Reading Module (47P)

Open-book plan-reading exam. Success comes from systematic verification, not memorization.

Typical verification sequence

  1. Review the structural notes and general notes first
  2. Confirm the statement of special inspections requirements
  3. Check formwork dimensions, elevations, and embeds against the drawings
  4. Verify reinforcement: size, spacing, cover, splices, hooks, and support
  5. Confirm that the concrete mix and testing frequency match the project requirements
  6. Document any discrepancy before concrete is placed

Common plan-reading traps: Missing a note that changes cover or splice length in a specific location, overlooking that a bar is called out as epoxy-coated, or failing to notice that the section cut does not match the plan view.
